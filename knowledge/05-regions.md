# Australian climate zones and degradation drivers

Two panels can deliver the same power today yet carry very different lifetime costs, because hidden material deterioration develops differently under each site's conditions. We tailor solar asset strategies to local environmental conditions and subsurface material evidence.

## Tropical north (Top End, Kimberley, far-north Queensland)
Intense rainfall, prolonged wetting, heat and humidity. Moisture ingress and encapsulant degradation dominate.

## Eastern seaboard (Queensland and NSW coasts)
Coastal salt deposits, bird activity, local shading. Salt and wetting affect connectors, fasteners, mounting structures and module edges; distance from the sea alone is not enough to judge exposure.

## Arid interior and north-west (Pilbara, Goldfields, Central Australia)
Strong diurnal temperature variation, mineral and mine dust deposits, seasonal dry periods. Thermal cycling stress and soiling dominate.

## Inland agricultural margins
Seasonal dry periods, intermittent rain, vegetation cycles, agricultural dust.

## Mediterranean-type south-west (south-west WA, SA)
Wet winters, extreme summer heat, episodic rainfall.

## Temperate south-east (Victoria, Tasmania, southern NSW)
Wetting, low solar angles, frost and occasional snow load.

## Exposure mechanisms we track
- Marine salt and wetting: salt deposition, wind direction, wetness duration.
- Ecology, deposition and land use: bird droppings, vegetation, agricultural dust, mine and road dust, ash, industrial deposits.
- Extreme weather and events: hail, cyclones, strong winds, flooding, wildfire heat and embers, local frost and snow. After an event we map the footprint and safe access first, then RGB damage mapping, then qualified electrical and structural review, with thermal only under valid conditions.
